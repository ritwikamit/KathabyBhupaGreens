# Content Writing Guidelines – Katha by Bhupa Greens

These rules apply to all website copy, menu descriptions, FAQs, blog posts, buttons and social snippets.

## 1. Write for a person who wants to visit

Use common words. Keep paragraphs short. Tell people where Katha is, what kind of food appears on the menu, how to find it and how to contact the restaurant.

Good: “Katha is inside the Bhupa Greens campus in Darbhanga. View the menu and use the map link to find the restaurant.”

Avoid: “Embark on a remarkable culinary journey through a vibrant world of flavours.”

## 2. Avoid AI-sounding vocabulary and filler

Avoid overused words and phrases such as:
- tapestry
- testament
- beacon
- delve
- pivotal
- paramount
- unwavering
- seamless / seamlessly
- foster / fostering
- dynamic
- vibrant
- holistic
- multifaceted
- culinary journey
- unforgettable experience
- where tradition meets innovation

Use the exact dish, ingredient, location or service instead.

## 3. Avoid formulaic writing

- Do not force every sentence into groups of three adjectives.
- Do not use constructions such as “not just food, but an experience.”
- Do not make a routine menu update sound like a major announcement.
- Do not add a generic summary paragraph at the end of every section.
- Do not repeat the restaurant name in every sentence to stuff keywords.

## 4. Stay within the facts

- Do not invent dish ingredients or cooking methods.
- Do not assume all dishes are available every day.
- Do not write “pure veg,” “halal,” “Jain,” “vegan,” “gluten-free” or allergen-free unless the kitchen confirms it.
- Do not invent opening hours, prices, discounts, table availability, delivery or awards.
- Do not make up customer quotes or reviews.
- Do not publish menu prices from an old third-party PDF without owner approval.

## 5. Tone and format

- Use simple English that is easy to understand.
- Prefer active voice.
- Use short, natural sentences.
- Use Indian English spelling where appropriate, such as “favourites”.
- Use “Katha” or “Katha by Bhupa Greens” consistently.
- Use “inside the Bhupa Greens campus” when explaining the location.
- Avoid excessive exclamation marks, emojis and decorative punctuation.
- Do not use em dashes. Use full stops, commas or brackets.

## 6. Two-pass copy check

Before publishing any text, check it twice.

**Pass 1 – Accuracy**
- Is every claim supported by the current menu or owner confirmation?
- Are the correct location and contact details used?
- Are prices, hours and services current?
- Are dietary/allergen details verified?

**Pass 2 – Human voice**
- Would a restaurant staff member say this naturally?
- Can any sentence be made shorter?
- Are there vague adjectives that can be replaced with a fact?
- Is the same point repeated?
- Does the copy sound like a real restaurant rather than a generic AI template?

If a fact is missing, leave a clear editorial note instead of guessing.
